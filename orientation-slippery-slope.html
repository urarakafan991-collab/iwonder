If two men, why not anything?

The comparison argument, and the line that does all the work.

This one runs exactly parallel to the race question on the other topic, so it gets the same treatment. Someone says: if you've accepted this, you've abandoned the principle that would let you refuse anything else. Then comes a list, usually children, animals, or family members.

It's worth answering properly rather than being offended by it, because the answer is clean, and because it turns out to be a principle the person making the argument already uses every day.

The line is consent, between adults capable of giving it. That single line does all of the work, and same-sex relationships sit plainly on one side of it.

1. Children

A child cannot consent. This is not a matter of degree or of social convention; it is a question of capacity, and it is the same reason a child cannot sign a contract, agree to surgery, or join the army.

There is an identifiable party who is harmed and who could not have agreed. Nothing in a relationship between two consenting adults resembles that in any respect.

2. Animals

Same answer, with an additional one stacked on top: no capacity to consent, and no mechanism by which consent could be expressed or withheld even in principle.

Again there is a party incapable of agreement. Again, two adults present no such problem.

3. Adult family members

This one is different, and it should be handled honestly rather than lumped in.

Consent can genuinely be present between adults here, so the objection has to rest on something else, and it does: genetic harm to any resulting children, and the difficulty of establishing clean consent inside a family power structure that predates the relationship and cannot be stepped out of.

Notice that those are specific, statable harms. The objection isn't “it's unusual” or “it's distasteful”. It names who is hurt and how. That is what a real objection looks like, and it's exactly what the case against same-sex relationships has never managed to produce.

4. What all three have in common

In every case there is either a participant who cannot consent, or an identifiable third party who is harmed.

Two adult men have neither. There is no one who hasn't agreed and no one being damaged. That is why the line holds, and it is not an arbitrary line: it is the line every legal system on earth already uses for everything else.

The principle was never “anything goes”. It was “adults who can consent, to each other”.

If you want to argue that same-sex relationships fail that test, you have to name the person who hasn't consented or the party being harmed. Nobody making this argument ever does, because there isn't one.

5. Two things worth noticing about the argument itself

The first is that it only ever runs in one direction. Nobody responds to an interracial marriage by asking what now stops us marrying a horse. Nobody raises it about second marriages, or marriages across religions, or couples with a large age gap between two adults. The slope is invoked selectively, which is a strong sign that the slope is not what is producing the objection.

The second is that the argument is self-defeating for almost everyone who reaches for it. If marriage is simply whatever a society decides it is, then yours has already changed it repeatedly: divorce and remarriage, marriage across faiths, marriage without a father's permission, marriage that a wife can leave. Every one of those was once the thing at the bottom of somebody's slope.

You already stopped somewhere, on grounds you consider good. So does everyone. That isn't being on a slippery slope. That's having a principle, and the principle available here is a much clearer one than the one you're currently using.
