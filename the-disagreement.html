Where the real disagreement sits

If you've read this far, you likely agree on more than you expected. The biological definition of sex. That dysphoria isn't a choice. That we already make social adjustments for conditions like CAIS without rewriting the definition.

The remaining disagreements are narrower and empirical: in which specific domains (elite sport, prisons, medical data) should males who have medically transitioned be included in female categories, and under what criteria? Those are policy questions that turn on evidence about effects, not on whether sex is real or whether dysphoria is chosen.

If you accept all of the above and still refuse basic social courtesy, then the disagreement was never about biology. It was about whether you think courtesy is owed to someone with a condition they didn't pick. That's a values question, not a scientific one.
