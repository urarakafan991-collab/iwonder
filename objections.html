Common objections

Eight objections to the argument, answered.

Common objections

"Men don't have vaginas. Women don't have penises."

If male means "internal reproductive system organized around sperm production, whether or not it functions," then a person with CAIS is male. That person has a vagina. So by this definition, a male person can have a vagina. "Men can have vaginas" is a conclusion that falls directly out of the framework being defended. If you don't like the conclusion, the problem is with the claim that the definition settles everything, not with the people who don't fit it.

"Chromosomes are the real definition, not gametes. XX and XY don't change."

Chromosomes fail the same test gametes do. A person with Swyer syndrome has XY chromosomes but a female reproductive system. Ovaries don't develop, but neither do testes, and the body follows a female pathway by default. A person with XX male syndrome has XX chromosomes but a male body, because an SRY gene fragment translocated onto an X chromosome. If chromosomes were the real criterion, both of these people would be misclassified by their own genetics failing to match their own bodies. Chromosomes are just another proxy that fails in the same predictable places gametes do, because no single marker settles the question as cleanly as it's assumed to.

"You admit sex is binary, then talk like it's a spectrum."

Nobody here said spectrum. The gamete definition is binary. What varies is the correlates we use as proxies: hormones, chromosomes, anatomy. Those don't always align, which is what intersex conditions show. That's "the proxies are imperfect," not "sex is a spectrum." The binary definition holds. It's just a heuristic, not a complete picture.

"People just prefer to present as the other gender. It's a choice."

"Prefer to present" is doing all the work and it hasn't been earned. Dysphoria is a diagnosed condition with biological contributors, not a preference someone picks. Calling it a preference doesn't make it one. If it's a preference, defend the word. Explain why someone would choose something that gets them disowned, harassed, and marginalized.

"Having a medical condition doesn't mean anything can be anything."

Nobody said anything can be anything. A dog that can't bark due to a condition is still a dog. Barking cannot be the criterion for being a dog. So if women produce eggs but some women don't produce eggs, producing eggs cannot be the criterion for being a woman. That's not self-declaration. That's acknowledging that conditions exist and categories have limits. This argument is about people diagnosed with a specific condition who take steps to medically transition, not "anyone who feels like it."

"A physical anomaly is easier to accommodate than a neurological one."

"Easier to accommodate" is not a criterion for whether accommodation is owed. It's just "I'd rather not." We accommodate neurological conditions constantly. ADHD, epilepsy, autism. The difficulty of accommodation has never been the standard.

"Just treat the condition medically. Why does society need to rearrange?"

Transition is the medical treatment. Social integration is part of why it works. Nobody is asking society to rearrange itself. The request is for the same social courtesy already extended to every other condition we accommodate.

"Having medical treatment is a choice. They chose to transition."

All medical treatment is a choice in the sense that you could refuse it and suffer instead. A diabetic chooses to take insulin. A person with poor eyesight chooses to wear glasses. Someone with a shattered leg chooses to get surgery and use a wheelchair. We don't refuse accommodation because the treatment was a decision. The entire concept of medical accommodation is built on the premise that when someone has a condition they didn't choose and undergoes treatment for it, we accommodate the treatment. Not because they were forced into it. Because the alternative is unnecessary suffering.

You wouldn't tell a cancer patient that because chemotherapy was their choice, accommodating their hair loss or immune suppression is optional. You wouldn't tell someone with a prosthetic leg that because the surgery was elective, treating them as someone who can walk is a favor they haven't earned.

If you concede that gender dysphoria is not chosen but argue that choosing to treat it disqualifies someone from accommodation, apply that to any other condition and hear how it sounds. That's not a principle. That's someone who ran out of reasons and is reaching for any technicality to justify a refusal they made before the conversation started.

And "it's just biology, accept it" is applied selectively. We correct biological realities through medicine every single day and accommodate those corrections socially without controversy. A person with poor eyesight has bad vision because of biology. We don't tell them to accept it. We give them glasses, and then we treat them as someone who can see. Hearing aids, prosthetics, insulin, pacemakers. Nobody says "that's just biology, deal with it" about any of those. The only time that line gets deployed is against trans people, and the only reason is that this particular correction bothers people while the others don't.
