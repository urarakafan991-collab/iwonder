Definitions

Two definitions the argument leaves open. Both accept everything on the other pages. They differ only on how wide the category should be. Neither is asserted here; pick whichever you find more defensible.

The first, with a medical requirement

Set one

Woman: An adult human who was assigned female at birth and has neither undergone nor intends to undergo masculinizing medical transition; or an adult human who was assigned male at birth who is treating gender dysphoria by having medically transitioned, or intending to medically transition, to a female-typical phenotype.

Man: An adult human who was assigned male at birth and has neither undergone nor intends to undergo feminizing medical transition; or an adult human who was assigned female at birth who is treating gender dysphoria by having medically transitioned, or intending to medically transition, to a male-typical phenotype.

This scopes the exception to people who are treating gender dysphoria through medical transition, rather than to self-identification alone. It has a clear, externally checkable boundary. It also leaves out people who are trans without access to treatment, or who do not want medical transition.

The second, without one

Set two

Woman: An adult human who stably lives as, and accepts themselves as belonging to, the social category their society builds around adult females rather than the one it builds around adult males.

Man: An adult human who stably lives as, and accepts themselves as belonging to, the social category their society builds around adult males rather than the one it builds around adult females.

This covers the people the first set leaves out, and does not tie the category to healthcare access. It is not circular: “woman” and “man” are defined by reference to the social categories a society builds around females and males, which are the biological terms, not by reference to themselves. It gives up the externally checkable boundary the first set has, since whether someone accepts a category as theirs is not something another person can measure.

Both sets accept everything argued on the other pages. The disagreement between them is not about whether the strict biological definition fails. It is about how wide the resulting category should be.
