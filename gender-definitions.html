Definitions

Two workable definitions, and the choice between them.

Identifying

Willingly associating with a gender category. Either from birth, combined with never having had a reason to leave, which describes around 99% of people in any category. Or associating with it to treat gender dysphoria, or in pursuit of gender euphoria.

Two definitions come out of the argument on the other pages. Both accept everything argued up to that point. They differ on one thing only, which is how wide the resulting category should be.

Pick whichever you find more defensible. The pages here do not insist on either.

The first, with a diagnostic requirement

Set one

A woman is an adult human person who is either biologically female and identifies with the female category, or someone who has a condition with biological or neurobiological traits, such as Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome or gender dysphoria, that results in them living as female.

A man is an adult human person who is either biologically male and identifies with the male category, or someone who has a condition with biological or neurobiological traits, such as gender dysphoria, that results in them living as male.

A girl is a young human person who is either biologically female and identifies with the female category, or someone who has a condition with biological or neurobiological traits, such as Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome or gender dysphoria, that results in them living as female.

A boy is a young human person who is either biologically male and identifies with the male category, or someone who has a condition with biological or neurobiological traits, such as gender dysphoria, that results in them living as male.

This is a transmedicalist framework. It scopes the exception to people with a diagnosed condition who take steps to transition medically, rather than to self-identification alone. It has a clear, externally checkable boundary. It also excludes people who are trans without a diagnosis, without access to treatment, or who do not want medical transition.

The second, without one

Set two

A woman is an adult human person who identifies with the female category, regardless of their biological sex.

A man is an adult human person who identifies with the male category, regardless of their biological sex.

A girl is a young human person who identifies with the female category, regardless of their biological sex.

A boy is a young human person who identifies with the male category, regardless of their biological sex.

This covers the people the first set leaves out, and does not tie the category to healthcare access. It gives up the diagnostic anchor that the earlier argument leans on.

Why neither is circular

“Woman” and “man” are defined in terms of “female” and “male”, which refer to the gamete-organisation classification. The second clause in the first set covers people with conditions, developmental or neurobiological, that result in them living as the other sex.

The phrase “identifies with the female category” is doing specific work. Without it, a trans man, biologically female and living as male, would technically qualify as a woman under the first clause. The identification clause keeps people who have moved away from their birth sex out of a definition they have moved beyond.

Both sets accept everything argued on the other pages. The disagreement between them is not about whether the strict biological definition fails. It is about how wide the resulting category should be.
