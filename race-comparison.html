Why race and gender transition are not parallel

The analogy only works against a definition this site does not use.

The argument runs: if a person can transition gender, why not race? If one is legitimate, so is the other; if the other is absurd, so is the first.

Start with what the analogy needs. It needs gender transition to rest on self-identification alone, so that anyone claiming any category is on the same footing. The definition on this site requires a diagnosed condition and medical transition undertaken or intended. Nobody with a diagnosis of race dysphoria exists, because no such diagnosis exists, because no such condition has ever been described in any clinical population. The analogy is aimed at a definition that is not on this site.

The specific differences are these.

  1. There is a condition. Gender dysphoria is a diagnosis in DSM-5-TR and, as gender incongruence, in ICD-11. It has a century of clinical description behind it and a prevalence in the general population of somewhere under one per cent. There is no recognised condition of race dysphoria, no clinical literature describing one, and no population of people presenting with it. The entire evidence base on the other side of the analogy is a handful of named individuals.
  2. There is a proposed mechanism. The leading hypothesis for gender dysphoria is that gender identity is shaped by prenatal hormone exposure interacting with genetic factors, along a trajectory that can diverge from the body's. It is a hypothesis with evidence behind it rather than a proof, and the first page says so. There is no proposed mechanism for race, because ancestry is not a system that develops in the body along any trajectory at all.
  3. There is a treatment with outcomes. For adults, transition is the recognised treatment in every major medical body's guidance, and regret after surgery runs at about 1% in the largest pooled review, with the honest caveat that the instruments varied and follow-up is incomplete in many of the underlying studies. No alternative has been shown to relieve the condition. For adolescents the evidence is weaker and contested, as set out elsewhere on this site. There is no treatment for race, because there is nothing it would treat.
  4. The categories are different kinds of thing. Sex is a biological classification with hormonal pathways that already exist in every body, which is why many sex characteristics can be medically altered. Race is a social classification laid over continuous genetic variation; the major genetics and anthropology bodies say so in their own position statements. There is no hormone of race and no pathway to act on.
  5. Ancestry is fixed and the body is not. Nothing changes who a person's ancestors were. A great deal changes the body, and hormones do it along routes the body already contains.

Put together: one side of the analogy is a diagnosed condition, a proposed biological mechanism, a treatment with measured outcomes and a body that can be altered. The other side is one or two famous cases, no condition, no mechanism, no treatment and nothing to alter. The analogy is not a reductio of gender transition. It is a demonstration that the person offering it has not looked at either half.

Sources

  1. DSM-5-TR and ICD-11, for the recognition of gender dysphoria and gender incongruence as diagnoses.
  2. Endocrine Society and NIH-supported reviews, on prenatal hormone exposure interacting with genetic factors as the leading hypothesis.
  3. Bustos et al. (2021), Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery Global Open, 27 studies and 7,928 patients, pooled regret 1% (95% CI under 1% to 2%); and the 2024 American Journal of Surgery review comparing it with regret after other procedures.
  4. American Medical Association, Endocrine Society and WPATH guidance on transition as recognised treatment in adults.
  5. The Cass Review (2024) and its commissioned University of York systematic reviews, on the state of the adolescent evidence.
  6. American Association of Biological Anthropologists and the American Society of Human Genetics, on race as a social classification over continuous variation.
  7. Endocrine Society, on sex characteristics being medically modifiable.

These are the bodies and papers behind each claim above. The specific figures have not all been individually traced; anyone relying on a number here should check it against the source.
