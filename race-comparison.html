Why race and gender transition are not parallel

Five specific differences, and what each one rests on.

Some people ask: if gender can be transitioned, why not race? Here's the actual difference.

  1. Gender dysphoria is a recognized medical condition involving clinically significant distress from a mismatch between gender identity and sex assigned at birth. There is no recognized medical condition of "race dysphoria."

Note: This distinction is important to keep in mind for point two. There's a difference between a regular hypothesis and a scientific hypothesis. A scientific hypothesis means this is where the majority of evidence points and what most scientists believe, which is different from a guess even before it's fully settled.

  1. The leading scientific hypothesis, where the overwhelming majority of evidence currently points, is that gender identity develops through prenatal hormone exposure interacting with genetic factors, with the brain and body developing along different trajectories. There is no equivalent process proposed for race, because ancestry isn't a system that develops in the body at all.
  2. Gender-affirming care is the almost universally recognized standard treatment for this condition, associated with the best outcomes and the lowest regret rates of any approach studied. Nothing has shown an alternative that produces better results. There is no equivalent treatment for race, because there's no condition it would be treating.
  3. Gender identity has documented neurobiological correlates. Race is a social construct based on ancestry and physical traits, with no neurological or hormonal basis behind it.
  4. Many sex characteristics are biologically modifiable through medical intervention, because the hormonal pathways involved already exist in the body. Genetic ancestry is fixed and cannot be altered by anything.

Gender transition is a recognized medical treatment for a documented condition with real biological underpinnings. "Race transition" has no medical basis, no recognized condition, and no biological mechanism behind it.
